## Community and Logic Model & Evidence Base (Tier and Quality) & Notice Priority & Member Experience

The Rural Georgia Youth Development Coalition, Inc. operates in Walker, Dade, and Catoosa counties, where rural isolation, limited access to supplemental academic support, and economic constraints contribute to persistent achievement gaps and heightened risk of school disengagement among students in grades 6‑12. Recent district data show that only 78 % of seniors in the target area graduate on time, and standardized assessments reveal that a substantial proportion of middle‑school learners perform below grade level in mathematics and reading. These conditions are exacerbated by a scarcity of after‑school programs and workforce‑readiness opportunities that could otherwise keep youth connected to school and prepare them for post‑secondary success. The Coalition’s AmeriCorps‑supported intervention directly addresses these challenges by placing eight full‑time members in four partner sites — LaFayette High School, Ridgeville Middle School, Trenton Elementary, and the Rossville Community Center — to deliver structured tutoring, summer bridge instruction, and monthly workforce‑readiness workshops.  

The logic model begins with the inputs required to implement the model: the Coalition’s existing facilities (headquarters and three school sites), the expertise of the program director (0.25 FTE) and site coordinators, curriculum materials purchased with the member training plan and tutoring curriculum budget line, and data‑collection systems funded through the evaluation line. Eight AmeriCorps members, each committing to a 1,700‑hour term (approximately 32 hours per week over a 32‑week program year), constitute the primary human resource. Members must possess a bachelor’s degree or equivalent experience in education, youth development, or a related field; demonstrate strong interpersonal and communication skills; exhibit cultural competence relevant to rural Northwest Georgia; and be able to adapt instructional strategies to diverse learning needs. Prior experience tutoring or mentoring adolescents is preferred, and all members must clear background checks and complete the Coalition’s pre‑service orientation.  

Core activities are delivered with a defined dosage to ensure consistency and intensity. Each member provides three 90‑minute tutoring sessions per week, three afternoons per week at their assigned site, for a total of 768 tutoring sessions annually (8 members × 3 sessions/week × 32 weeks). The summer bridge program operates four weeks at 20 hours per week, delivering 80 hours of intensive academic enrichment per site. Workforce‑readiness workshops are held monthly, yielding 12 sessions per year that cover resume building, interview skills, career exploration, and soft‑skill development.  

The target population consists of students in grades 6‑12 residing in Walker, Dade, and Catoosa counties who are identified as at risk of disengaging from school based on attendance, behavior, or academic performance indicators. The Coalition aims to serve approximately 420 youth annually — consistent with its historical reach — across the four sites. Measurable outputs include the 768 tutoring sessions, the 80‑hour summer bridge experience, and the 12 workforce workshops. In addition, the program will engage an estimated 60 leveraged community volunteers who assist with workshop facilitation and enrichment activities, further expanding the reach of each output.  

These activities are designed to produce a cascade of outcomes. In the short term, participants are expected to demonstrate measurable gains in foundational academic skills, specifically an increase of at least 0.4 grade equivalents in mathematics and 0.5 grade equivalents in reading, as evidenced by pre‑ and post‑assessment data collected each program year. In the medium term, improved academic performance is anticipated to translate into higher school engagement, reflected in increased attendance (target: 85 % of youth attending 30 + sessions per year) and improved school‑engagement scores (target: 86 % of regularly attending youth showing gains). Long‑term outcomes focus on educational attainment and workforce readiness: the Coalition projects that 90 % of regularly attending seniors will graduate on time, surpassing the district baseline of 78 %, and that 70 % of graduating participants will enroll in post‑secondary education or secure employment within six months of graduation. These projections are grounded in the Coalition’s internal evaluation of its existing programming, which documented comparable gains among participants who received similar tutoring and mentoring supports.  

Evidence Base  
The Coalition submits its 2025 internal evaluation as the primary evidence document. This study employed a quasi‑experimental pre/post design with a matched comparison group drawn from district‑wide records. The analytic sample comprised 387 youth who received at least 30 hours of tutoring or mentoring services over a three‑year period. Academic outcomes were measured using standardized assessments converted to grade‑equivalent scores; graduation status was obtained from school transcripts. Key findings indicated that participants achieved average gains of +0.4 grade equivalents in mathematics and +0.5 grade equivalents in reading relative to baseline. Furthermore, 91 % of regularly attending seniors in the cohort graduated on time, compared with 78 % of demographically similar peers in the district comparison group. These results demonstrate that the Coalition’s tutoring‑centric model produces meaningful improvements in both academic proficiency and graduation likelihood.  

Complementary evidence is drawn from the Coalition’s routine performance‑measure data reported in its 2024‑25 program report. That analysis showed that 87 % of youth attended 30 + sessions of service, and 84 % of regularly attending youth exhibited improved school‑engagement scores as measured by the Coalition’s locally validated engagement instrument. While these data lack an external comparison group, they corroborate the internal evaluation’s pattern of high dosage receipt and positive intermediate outcomes.  

Taken together, the internal evaluation’s quasi‑experimental design with a comparison group and the consistent performance‑measure trends satisfy the criteria for a Moderate evidence tier under the Corporation for National and Community Service framework. The intervention evaluated — small‑group, dosage‑rich tutoring supplemented by summer bridge and workforce workshops — aligns precisely with the proposed AmeriCorps program, ensuring that the evidence base directly informs the expected outcomes.  

Notice Priority  
The Georgia Serves Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO A.2) identifies improving academic outcomes for at‑risk youth and strengthening workforce readiness as funding priorities. The Coalition’s program directly advances both priorities: the tutoring and summer bridge components target measurable increases in mathematics and reading proficiency and on‑time graduation rates, while the monthly workforce‑readiness workshops explicitly aim to equip participants with the skills, credentials, and career awareness necessary for successful transition to employment or post‑secondary education. By integrating academic remediation with career exploration, the project addresses the dual goals outlined in the solicitation, making the alignment a significant and intentional facet of the program design.  

Member Experience  
AmeriCorps members will receive a structured sequence of professional development and reflective experiences intended to foster skill attainment, personal growth, and enduring civic connection. Upon selection, members complete a 40‑hour pre‑service orientation that covers youth‑development theory, trauma‑informed tutoring techniques, cultural humility specific to rural Georgia, and ethical service standards. Throughout the service year, members participate in bi‑weekly coaching sessions led by the program director and site coordinators, focusing on instructional refinement, data‑driven decision making, and personal goal setting. The member retention budget line funds certifications (e.g., CPR/First Aid, youth‑mental‑health first aid), recognition events, and stipends for attending state‑level AmeriCorps trainings, thereby reinforcing both competence and a sense of belonging within the national service community.  

Members also engage in structured reflection activities after each tutoring block and workforce workshop, guided by prompts that link their service experiences to broader civic responsibilities. These reflections are compiled into a service portfolio that members may use for future academic or employment applications. Additionally, the Coalition facilitates quarterly community‑forum events where members present outcomes, solicit feedback from parents and school partners, and collaborate with the 60 leveraged volunteers on service‑learning projects such as college‑access fairs and STEM demonstration days. Through these mechanisms, members not only hone practical skills in education and youth mentorship but also develop leadership, communication, and problem‑solving abilities that support a lifetime of civic participation.
