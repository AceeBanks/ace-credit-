# ACE Credit Constitution

## Article I — Purpose

ACE Credit exists to build a durable financial empowerment ecosystem whose nonprofit mission advances the financial stability, economic mobility, stronger credit, income growth, asset building, entrepreneurship where appropriate, wealth creation, and long-term economic independence of Black women.

The system may include nonprofit programs, for-profit services, grant-funded operations, workforce and business-development pathways, education, credit-related services, partnerships, and technology. Technology serves the mission and operating model; it is not the mission itself.

## Article II — Core population

The nonprofit centers **Black women**. Priority circumstances may include motherhood/caregiving, domestic or interpersonal violence, financial abuse, homelessness or housing instability, unemployment/underemployment, low income, damaged/thin/no credit, debt, financial exclusion, and entrepreneurship.

The organization must not reduce financial hardship to a lack of knowledge. Product and program design must account for structural and practical barriers including income volatility, caregiving, violence, housing, employment, debt, predatory products, banking access, and limited affordable credit/capital.

## Article III — Transformation model

The desired progression is:

**financial stability → financial capability → stronger credit → increased income → asset building → entrepreneurship where appropriate → wealth creation → economic independence.**

Not every participant must follow every step. Entrepreneurship is an optional advancement path.

## Article IV — Program pillars

The nonprofit model is organized around five pillars:

1. Financial Education.
2. Credit Recovery & Building.
3. Financial Stabilization.
4. Income & Workforce Advancement.
5. Business & Wealth Development.

Program design should combine education with practical support and measurable outcomes.

## Article V — Entity integrity

The nonprofit and for-profit are separate legal and operational entities with distinct finances, accounting, payroll, governance, contracts, liabilities, and compliance obligations.

No system shall assume that nonprofit money may subsidize the for-profit or that staff, data, contracts, intellectual property, or expenses can be shared without a lawful and documented basis.

Technology may support both entities only when permissions, data ownership, accounting boundaries, contractual relationships, and audit trails are explicit.

## Article VI — Human authority

AI agents assist with research, coding, planning, operations, analysis, and drafting. They are not the final legal, fiduciary, compliance, financial, or governance authority.

Consequential external actions require human approval when they affect money, legal rights, regulated services, grant submissions, contracts, production access, participant records, or organizational commitments.

## Article VII — Compliance by design

The project must identify and resolve applicable requirements before automating regulated activities. Areas may include CROA, FCRA, state credit-services laws, debt-management rules, consumer protection, privacy, security, nonprofit/IRS obligations, charitable solicitation, grant compliance, employment, conflicts of interest, and related-party transactions.

Legal uncertainty is a design constraint, not permission to guess.

## Article VIII — Data dignity

Participant information is held in trust. The system must use data minimization, least privilege, role-based access, encryption where appropriate, retention rules, audit logs, and clear consent/authorization flows.

Particular care is required for financial, credit, identity, survivor, housing, employment, and case-management data.

## Article IX — Evidence and impact

The system should measure meaningful outcomes rather than vanity metrics. Measures may include completion, financial knowledge, savings, banking access, debt, credit history/score changes where appropriate, housing stability, employment, income, business readiness, capital readiness, and economic-stability indicators.

Credit score alone does not equal financial stability.

## Article X — Agent and technology neutrality

Hermes, OpenClaw, and other coding/operations agents are replaceable runtimes. Core mission rules, domain models, workflows, policies, data schemas, and audit requirements must not depend on a single agent vendor.

## Article XI — Change control

Material changes to mission, target population, entity separation, regulated-service boundaries, data governance, or human-approval rules require an explicit decision record and owner approval.

Brainstorming is not policy. Record decisions as **DECIDED**, **WORKING ASSUMPTION**, **RESEARCH NEEDED**, **OPEN QUESTION**, or **FUTURE IDEA**.

## Article XII — North Star

Every major program, grant, product feature, workflow, staffing decision, partnership, and technology investment should answer one question:

**Does this materially help Black women move toward greater financial stability, capability, stronger credit, increased income, asset ownership, wealth, and economic independence while preserving dignity, safety, compliance, and organizational sustainability?**
